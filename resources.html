What is Implicit Bias?

Implicit bias refers to attitudes or assumptions that can affect the way we view and interact with other people without us being fully aware of them. These biases can develop from our experiences, surroundings, culture, and the information we are exposed to throughout our lives. Even when someone does not intentionally discriminate against others, implicit biases can still influence decisions and behavior.

Implicit Bias in Technology

Implicit bias is important to consider when developing technology because the people who design digital systems make decisions about the data, features, and problems those systems are means to address. If training data doesn't adequately represent different populations, an AI system may perform less accurately for certain groups. For example, research from the National Institute of Standard and Technology (NIST) found demographic differences in the performance of many facial recognition algorithms. This demonstrates that technology shouldn't automatically be considered objective just because it uses data and algorithms.

These problems can have consequences beyond a technical error. Facial recognition may be used for identity verification or identification, meaning inaccurate results can affect people's access to services or lead to additional scrutiny. Developers should test systems across different demographic groups and consider how errors could affect the people using them.

Why Intersectionality Matters in Technology

Intersectionality helps us understand why technology may not affect everyone in the same way. A person's experiences can be shaped by several overlapping disability, and socioeconomic background. Looking at only one characteristic may overlook the different challenges people experience when these identities intersect.

For example, when evaluating a facial recognition system, researchers should examine whether its performance differs across both racial and gender groups instead of relying only on an overall accuracy score. A system might perform well on average while still produciing more errors for a specific group. This shows why testing technology across diverse populations is important.

Intersectionality also encourages developers to consider whose experiences are represented during the design process. Including people with different backgrounds can help identify problems that might otherwise be overlooked. However, diverse representation alone doesn't guarantee fairness. Developers must also test their systems, evaluate their assumptions, and address unequal outcomes.

What is Intersectionality?

Intersectionality is the idea that different parts of a person's identity and experiences can overlap and influence the way they experience the world. Factors such as race, gender, social class, and other parts of someone's background do not always affect people separately. Looking at intersectionality can help explain why people with different combinations of identities may experience opportunities, challenges, or discrimination differently.

Harvard Implicit Bias Test

Harvard's Project Implicit provides several Implicit Association Tests that allow people to explore possible automatic associations they may have. The tests can be a useful starting point for thinking about implicit bias and reflecting on where our own assumptions may come from.

It is important to remember that an IAT result doesn't define a person's character or prove that they will discriminate against others. The test measures the strength of automatic associations, and results can be influenced by context and other factors. Project Implicit describes the IAT primarily as a tool for reflection and learning rather than a diagnostic test. Its results are best used as a starting point for thinking critically about assumptions, rather than as a final judgement about an individual.

Moving Toward More Inclusive Technology

Understanding implicit bias and intersectionality is only the first step toward creating more inclusive technology. Developers and organizations should examine the data they use, test their systems across different populations, and listen to feedback from people who may be affected by their decisions. They should also be transparent about the limitations of their systems and take responsibility when problems are identified.

Ultimately, fairness in technology requires more than good intentions. It requires ongoing evaluation of how systems work in practice and who benefits or experiences harm from their use. By considering different perspectives and questioning assumptions, we can better understand how technology can reinforce existing inequalities or help reduce them.
